RescueTime alternatives in 2026: free and open source options compared

November 24, 2026

RescueTime made automatic time tracking popular: install it, forget it, and see where the day went. People look for alternatives for three reasons: the price of the paid plan, not wanting their activity stored in someone else's cloud, or wanting something they can inspect and change. This is a comparison of the main options, written by the people who make one of them, so we have tried to be plain about where each one is better than ours.

RescueTime

The reference. It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS, its categorization of sites is mature, and its reports are polished. The free Lite plan tracks automatically; focus sessions and the fuller reports are in Premium. Your activity is uploaded to RescueTime's servers, which is what makes the multi-device view work and also what some people want to avoid.

Choose it if you want something finished that works on every device, and the cloud does not bother you.

ActivityWatch

The best-known open source tracker, and a good one. It is free, runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, and keeps everything on your machine. It is built around "watchers" and a local web interface, with a query language for people who like to dig. It asks for a little more setup, and categories are rules you write yourself.

Choose it if you use macOS or Linux, or want the most established open source option.

ManicTime

A Windows-first tracker with a very detailed timeline of your day, aimed at people who bill by the hour and need to reconstruct what they did. The free version tracks and tags; the paid license adds the rest. Data stays local unless you use their server or cloud.

Choose it if you bill clients and want a precise timeline to tag.

Toggl Track

Not really the same kind of tool: Toggl is built on timers you start and stop, by project and client, with strong team reporting. It is excellent at that. It will not tell you that you spent ninety minutes on YouTube unless you time it yourself.

Choose it if what you need is timesheets by project, not a picture of your habits.

zman

zman is ours: free, open source, written in Rust, and local-first. It tracks apps and websites automatically, gives you categories, a productivity score and focus blocks, blocks distracting sites with focus sessions and daily limits, and has one thing the others do not: your AI assistant can read it. Connect Claude with one click and ask "where did my week go?" in plain language.

What it does not have yet: it is a young project, it runs on Windows only (with an Android companion), and it has no team features out of the box.

Choose it if you are on Windows, want your data on your own computer, and like the idea of asking instead of reading charts.

How to choose

  • Every device, no setup: RescueTime.
  • Open source on macOS or Linux: ActivityWatch.
  • Billing by the hour: ManicTime or Toggl Track.
  • Windows, private, with AI and site blocking: zman.

Most freelancers know what they charge. Very few know what they earn per hour. Those are two different numbers, and the gap between them explains the months when you worked a lot and the money is not there.

The rate you quote and the rate you earn

Your quoted rate is what you tell the client: so much an hour, or so much for the project. Your real rate is what is left when you divide what you were paid by all the hours that client actually took.

Real rate = what you were paid ÷ all the hours you spent

The catch is "all the hours". When you quote, you count the hours of doing the work. But a client also takes hours nobody bills:

  • Messages and emails going back and forth.
  • Meetings, and the time before and after each one.
  • The fixes that "would take a minute".
  • Writing the quote and the invoice, and chasing the payment.
  • Getting back into the project after two weeks away from it.

What to do with the number

The real rate per client helps with four concrete decisions:

  1. Whose price to raise. The one far below your average. You have the fact to explain it: "this work takes twice what we estimated".
  2. What kind of work to look for. See what the clients at the top of the table have in common: the industry, the kind of project, how they communicate.
  3. How much to ask next time. Quote with the real hours of the last similar project, not the ones you imagine.
  4. When to charge a retainer. If a client takes many small, scattered hours, a monthly retainer with a cap on hours brings order for both of you.

The hard part is measuring the hours

The sum is easy. What is hard is having the real hours, because nobody writes down ten minutes of chat or the fix at eleven at night. There are three ways to get them:

  • Writing them down in a spreadsheet at the end of the day. It is free, and almost nobody keeps it up for more than two weeks.
  • Timers such as Toggl or Clockify. They work if you remember to start them and to switch them every time you change client.
  • Automatic tracking. A program sees which window you are in and adds the time up by itself. It does not depend on your memory.

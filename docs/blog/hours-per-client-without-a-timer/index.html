How to know the hours each client takes, without a timer

November 26, 2026

If you ever tried to measure your hours with a timer, you know how it ends: the first week you always start it, the second you forget half the time, and by the third you have a report even you do not believe. The problem is not your discipline. The method depends on remembering one thing while you are focused on another.

Why timers fail

  • You have to start them. And the moment you begin working is exactly when you least want to open another app.
  • You have to switch them. A freelancer jumps between clients many times a day. Every jump is a click you forget.
  • They miss the small stuff. Nobody starts a timer to answer a three-minute message. Added up, those messages are hours.
  • They get fixed from memory. By the time you notice it ran all afternoon, the number is a guess.

The alternative: let the computer know by itself

Your computer already knows what you are working on, because it knows which window is in front. And that window nearly always says who the work is for:

What you doWhat the window says
CodingThe project folder, in the title of the editor or the terminal
Designing or writingThe file name, which usually carries the client's name
Using the client's systemThe site's address in the browser
Meetings and emailThe company name in the subject or the title

An automatic tracker reads that and assigns each minute to a client with simple rules. There is nothing to start and nothing to switch.

Setting it up in zman, step by step

  1. Install zman and leave it running. It starts tracking by itself. It is free and the data stays on your computer.
  2. Work normally for a couple of days. Do not set anything up yet.
  3. Open "Clients" in the dashboard. zman shows the project folders it saw that have no client: click one and it is created.
  4. Fill in each client's rules: a word that shows up in the window title, their website, or an app you only use for them.
  5. Mark your own projects. They count hours, but stay out of what you earn per hour.
  6. Enter what each one paid when they pay. That is when the real rate per hour appears.

Rules also apply backwards: if you add a client today, zman assigns it the hours it had already tracked.

What to check every week

  • The time with "no client". It is what matched nobody. If it is a lot, a rule is missing or there is a client you have not added. Some will always remain: that is your admin time, and it is worth knowing how much it is.
  • The client that took the most hours. Is it also the one that pays the most?
  • Hours against the quote. If a project has used the hours you estimated and is half done, it is better to talk about it now than at the end.

What zman does not see

To be clear: automatic tracking measures what happens on the computer. A meeting in person, a phone call or the time you think a problem through while walking do not show up. And chats where you serve several clients in the same window, like WhatsApp Web, are counted as time with no client today. Even so, the number you get is much closer to reality than one from a timer you forgot to start.
